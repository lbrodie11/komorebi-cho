# 09 — Animation & feel

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

---

## Goal
Make the tiny people and objects *feel* good at 10 pixels tall. Juice without noise.

## Tasks
1. **Procedural rig.** Add arms (two boxes) with counter-swing, head bob, a slight torso lean into turns, and turn smoothing (max yaw rate). Kids bounce more. The jogger gets a run cycle and the elderly a slower gait with a cane.
2. **Interruption contracts.** Every action declares markers (`Seated → StandBegin → WeightTransfer → FreeLocomotion`) and an interrupt policy (immediately / at marker / must complete). If a seat vanishes, an invisible action proxy keeps it until FreeLocomotion. Write tests for remove-while-seated and remove-while-in-doorway.
3. **Doors.** Doors and shutters animate open and closed when someone enters or leaves. Residents emerging from a door step out of it instead of appearing. A brief dither-in (Bayer threshold over 6 frames) can hide the hand-off.
4. **Acting details:**
   - sweeping (dust puffs), wiping tables, crouch-gardening (soil particles)
   - praying with a clap (sound and a 2-frame hand pose), buying from a vending machine (a can drops; clunk sound)
   - reading on a bench, phone glances for the commuter
5. **World juice:**
   - Placement squash-and-stretch with dust motes.
   - Removal shrinks with a puff.
   - Pigeons get two flap frames and land with a hop.
   - Cats stretch on waking and their tails flick.
   - Koi surface occasionally, with ripple rings.
   - Lanterns sway with the wind.
   - Laundry flutters harder in rain.
   - Parasols fold.
6. **Camera feel:**
   - Zoom steps ease with a slight overshoot.
   - Panning has gentle inertia (disabled with reduced motion).
   - Rotation uses the crossfade from prompt 02.

## Acceptance criteria
- At the default zoom, a viewer can tell walking, sitting, sweeping, praying and gardening apart in a 5-second clip.
- There are no pops: every appearance and disappearance is motivated (a door, an exit, a dither).
- The juice audit: list every motion in the game, then cut anything that draws the eye without meaning.

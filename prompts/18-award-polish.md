# 18 — Award polish

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

## Goal
The last 10% that people remember.

## Tasks
1. **The first 90 seconds, scripted beat by beat.** Write it down, then make it true:
   - 0–10 s (16:36): the island fades up, golden hour, sound prompt
   - 10–30 s: the eye lands on the kissaten window, a cat stretches
   - 30–60 s: the bench hint, placement pop, a resident notices
   - 54–90 s: lamps start to come on (17:30); the evening bell rings at about 84 s (18:00); blue hour follows at about 2 min 40 s (19:15)

   Time it with three strangers.
2. **Juice audit.** List every animation and sound and rate each one. Cut what's noise and amplify what carries meaning.
3. **Consistency audit:**
   - palette usage (no stray colours)
   - outline weights
   - icon pixel grid
   - copy tone (sentence case, warm, unvarnished)
   - timing curves (one easing family)
4. **"Things to discover" list.** At least 25 small secrets, all deterministic and code-only. For example:
   - the cat that sits on the islet at full moon
   - the bookseller reading the same book for a week
   - Genzo's bell timing
   - a lost glove on the bridge railing in winter
   - the kissaten radio changing songs at 3 pm
5. **Bug bash.** Two hours with five people. Fix every crash, every visual pop and every confusing moment.
6. **Performance last pass** on the lowest-spec target device.
7. **Credits screen** listing everyone and every inspiration.

8. **The signature image: komorebi.** The game is named after sunlight through leaves, so show it. Done: tree canopies cast dappled shadows. Their shadow pass cuts holes from a noise field measured across the sun's beam, so every blob along a ray agrees and light comes through the whole canopy; it drifts with the wind, is stable while the camera pans, rotates and zooms, and only canopies get it (shrubs and pots stay solid). It snaps to the palette in the pixel looks, stills under reduced motion, and cost no measurable frame time. Still to do: tune patch size and density per season (bare winter branches let most light through; summer is densest), and capture the day image (dappled street at 15:00) beside the night one (the blue-hour kissaten) for the store page and the press kit.
9. **Contact depth, stable.** Not screen-space AO (see RESEARCH-NOTES): extend `weathering.ts`'s per-vertex contact darkening to props and placeables, and give residents, benches and planters small soft blob shadows (a decal, palette-snapped). Check the base of a shopfront, a tree's roots and someone standing by a bench, before and after.
10. **Art Director Mode** (dev builds only, behind `import.meta.env.DEV`, never shipped). A panel that edits the real data, not vague knobs: the sky keyframes (`KEYS` in `sky.ts`), `EMISSIVE` and `ACCENT_BUDGET` in `lighting.ts`, the seasonal colour tables, `KOMOREBI` in `materials.ts`. It runs on a frozen, repeatable frame (`__komorebi.freeze`) with a before/after toggle and a Smooth / Classic switch (palette snapping changes how values read), and exports a TS/JSON diff to paste back into the source, so no tuning lives only in localStorage. Presets to compare: rainy dusk, summer afternoon, snowy morning, late night.
11. **Material identity, tested in the pixel looks.** Roof tiles, wet stone and aged wood should answer the same light differently (a few broad highlights on wet tile, diffuse plaster beside it). A broad highlight snaps to one or two palette colours and can land as a blob, and placed lanterns are ground-pool decals, not real lights, so they light no surfaces: check every change in Classic, not only Smooth.

**Rules for any new visual effect:** a frame-time number at Smooth (about 4× Classic's pixels) on a laptop's integrated GPU, not only a good screenshot; bake into vertices or the shadow map rather than sampling per screen pixel, which shifts on rotate and zoom; and rerun the hierarchy check, since new bright points bloom in Film Diffusion and the kissaten must stay the brightest thing at 19:15.

12. **An optional guided tour** of three or four places (Sunday Café, the park, Sunbeam Bathhouse, the lantern-lit shopping street), each a composed view with a moment of stillness. Always skippable, never automatic, and a set of cuts (no glides) under reduced motion.

13. **Measure what the hook promises,** in the playtest:
    - **Causal recognition rate:** of the bond stories shown, how often the player correctly names what they did.
    - **Two Likert questions:** "I felt the neighborhood remembered what I did" and "the residents acted for themselves".
    - **Complaints** that stories repeat.
    - **A Journal wording A/B:** factual ("Haru sat on the bench three evenings") against causal ("Since you placed the bench, Haru has stopped here three evenings"). Judge on trust and repetitiveness, not just preference.
14. **Reasons to return, never pressure.** The almanac line, "while you were away", festivals and paths worn since last time. No streaks, no daily rewards, no timers. The genre's typical playtime is one to five hours, mostly because building has no consequence; ours should.

15. **Readability audit** (from the 2026-11-11 pixel-readability review):
    - At Normal, no HUD text is smaller than 12 CSS px (some notes are 10–11 px today).
    - Every panel works in both letterings (Pixel and Plain) and at all four text sizes, on a 390×844 phone and a 1366×768 laptop. Rows wrap; nothing overlaps or clips.
    - The painted 3×5 signs read at the default zoom in Classic. Check M against W and N against H; widen to 4×5 where a word fails.
    - In the Pixel lettering, I, l and 1 and O and 0 are distinct where they appear (times, the date, counts).

16. **Desktop discoverability.** Every camera move has a visible control as well as a key. The street turns with Q / E and with the two turn buttons above the zoom buttons (added 2026-11-11). Check that a new player finds rotation within the first two minutes without opening Help; if not, add a first-minutes hint.

## Acceptance criteria
- 3 of 3 strangers say "oh!" at the first resident reaction, without prompting.
- Median session in the playtest is over 20 minutes.
- Zero known crashes, and zero visible pops in the first hour.
- Causal recognition rate of 80% or more in the playtest.

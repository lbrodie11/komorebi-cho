# 10b — Light and sky: four times of day you can tell apart

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

## Goal
Dawn, midday, sunset and night should each be unmistakable at a glance, and midday (the game is named for sunlight through leaves) should be the most alive, not the flattest. The drama comes from light inside a safe, calm place. It never comes from threat: no storm fronts, no spotlit darkness.

Run this with or straight after prompt 10. Sources and the claims that didn't hold up are in `RESEARCH-NOTES.md` under "Light and sky review".

## Tasks
1. **Measure first.** A script (extend `scripts/diffusion-shots.mjs`) captures 06:00, 12:00, 18:00 and 21:30 at the same seed and camera and prints, for each:
   - mean luminance of sunlit against shaded grass;
   - wall luminance and hue;
   - plinth against the backdrop directly below it;
   - how many lit windows.

   The targets below are checked from these numbers, not by eye alone.
2. **Midday: real komorebi contrast.** Canopy dapples exist (prompt 18, task 8), but sunlit and shaded grass sit close together, so noon reads as overcast.
   - Target at least 2.5:1 luminance between a dapple and the shade around it at summer noon (about 1.3:1 was measured from screenshots), using palette colors: a warm yellow-green in the light, a cool deep green in the shade.
   - Shade leans cool and light leans warm. Under-eave shade gets deeper at noon.
   - A warm one-pixel fringe on dapple edges is an experiment only. Adopt it if it reads as sunlight in Classic and costs no measurable frame time.
3. **Sky with shapes, in the game's own pixels.** Today's cloud bands are a soft full-resolution smudge.
   - In the palette looks, draw clouds on the render grid in two or three palette tones with a dithered edge. In Smooth they may stay soft.
   - **By time and season:**
     - a tall summer cumulus behind the bathhouse chimney at midday;
     - thin pale streaks at dawn;
     - under-lit bands at sunset;
     - one or two faint wisps across the moon at night.
   - Clouds stay **above** the horizon. None go under the plinth: this is a model on a stand, not a floating island.
   - The moon is drawn on the render grid in the palette looks, a crisp pixel crescent, and follows prompt 10's phases.
4. **Ground the plinth.** The backdrop gradient runs the full height, so by day the brightest pixels on screen are under the dark wood, and at night the plinth melts into the sky.
   - Prototype a horizon split: sky above, and below a quiet, low-contrast surface (a table or alcove tone), with a soft contact shadow under the plinth and a faint highlight on its top edge.
   - Judge it A/B against today's backdrop in all four times and both looks. The plinth must separate from what's below it (at least 1.3:1 at night), and nothing below the horizon may be brighter than the sky above it.
   - This changes the signature image, so it ships only if the A/B is clearly better. The decision is the owner's.
5. **Night: three tiers of light, and windows that follow people.**
   - **Focal glow:** the café, the shop fronts and the bathhouse entrance, warm.
   - **Homes:** dimmer and softer. Each window follows its resident's own bedtime, so upstairs lights go out one by one between 21:00 and midnight, and nothing goes dark all at once. Check `lighting.ts`'s window schedules against the personas' `sleep` hours.
   - **Street lamps:** cool, small pools.
   - The kissaten stays the brightest thing at 19:15 (the hierarchy check).
   - **The pond:** vertical, dithered reflections of the lanterns and the bridge on the water at night.
6. **The bathhouse keeps hours.** No steam in the morning. A faint heat shimmer at the chimney in the early afternoon (the boiler). Soft white steam from the high windows while it's open, from mid-afternoon to late evening. The entrance curtain is lit while it's open. Steam is thicker in winter (prompt 10).
7. **Low-sun shadows that stay attached.** At dawn and dusk the sun is low, and shadow bias grows with the cotangent of its elevation. Keep the sun's color from its true elevation but clamp the shadow-casting elevation to about 10°, so shadows are long but never detach from feet and bench legs. The shadow frustum is already fitted (`shadow.ts`); check for swimming at 06:00 and 18:00 while panning.
8. **Small water, human scale.**
   - The stone basin placeable (`waterBasin`) gets a bamboo spout and a slow drip (a sound, and a triangulator in prompt 09b). Sparrows bathe in it (prompt 09c).
   - **Sprinkling the street:** on dry summer mornings and evenings a shopkeeper throws water on the lane with a ladle. Dark patches dry over a few minutes. A real shitamachi habit, and a routine worth noticing.
   - The existing small waterfall on the terrace wall stays as it is (the park that inspired ours has one). Nothing pours off the plinth.
9. **Blue hour as its own moment.** Between sunset and night the sky is deep blue while the windows are warm. That is the benchmark scene (the café at blue hour). Make sure the keys around 19:15 hold that contrast for several in-game minutes, in every season.

## Not doing (and why)
- **Clouds or a "sea of clouds" under the plinth; water falling off the edge.** That turns a model on a stand into a fantasy sky island.
- **Glowing trees, spirit stones, a wall of skyscrapers behind the street.** Wrong place and wrong scale for a low backstreet.
- **Dimming 80% of the lights for one "solitary hearth".** Fourteen people live here; an empty-looking town is the wrong story. Hierarchy, not blackout.
- **Storm-front tension.** Soft fascination (leaves, water, drifting clouds) restores attention; threat doesn't.
- **Distance fog over the diorama.** It's 30 m across; haze would only grey it. Mist stays a weather state.
- **Health claims** such as waterfalls and "negative ions" boosting serotonin. A screen emits none, and a 2013 meta-analysis found no consistent effect on mood anyway.

## Acceptance criteria
- Four screenshots (06:00, 12:00, 18:00, 21:30), unlabeled and in greyscale: 5 of 5 people put them in the right order.
- Summer noon: dapple to shade at least 2.5:1; under-eave shade visibly deeper than at 09:00.
- Dawn and sunset differ in sun warmth, shade hue and lamp level (the test), and in cloud shape.
- At 23:30 fewer than half the home windows are lit, and they went out one at a time.
- No frame-time increase over 0.3 ms at Smooth on the reference laptop; the hierarchy check still passes at every diffusion level.

# 08 — Resident AI v2

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

## Goal
Residents who feel like *people with lives*, and whose adaptation to the player's edits is unmistakable.

Done (the thin slice, task 9): `src/sim/places.ts` place memory: familiarity in days of use with a two-day half-life, favourites as habits (3 days) with a 15% hysteresis margin, novelty that pulls the curious for a few days. Choices weigh familiarity, the favourite and novelty. Mitsuko's pond bench is a haunt (persona data). The three-day arc runs on its own: she tries a new bench, comes back another day ("Mitsuko tries the new bench again"), and adopts it ("Mitsuko has a new favorite spot: the bench"). Remove it and she goes to look for it (`?`, "Mitsuko looks for the bench. It's gone."), then settles on another. The card shows "Favorite spot: the pond bench". The cats settle within 2 m of her while she sits. Place memory is saved. The town's benches have names of their own (the pond bench, the bench under the sakura). Tests: the unit rules, and the whole arc headless (four seeds checked), including removal and the cats.

Done (second pass):
- **Utility** (`src/sim/utility.ts`): needs through response curves (logistic tiredness, quadratic thirst), weight × considerations with Dave Mark's compensation, a mood drawn per kind of activity (variety without dithering), a 15% hysteresis margin for what was chosen last of each kind, satiation (a drink just had makes another unappealing for an hour), and decisions staggered 0.5–2 s apart. Test: two benches 3% apart never flip-flop over 400 decisions.
- **Needs:** fatigue, thirst and now social (company), which chatting satisfies.
- **Social layer:** friendships in persona data (the kids, Mitsuko and Genzo, the shopkeepers). Neighbours passing close bow; friends a few meters away, not about to pass, wave; friends on one bench chat in alternating bubbles (told once a day). Friends make a place inviting instead of crowded, so the kids end up playing together.
- **Diaries** (`src/sim/diary.ts`): one line a day per resident, written at midnight from the day's most notable real event, its weather and a seeded detail never repeated two days running ("Tuesday — the bench is my spot now. Rain. The bell at six."). The last two show on the card; a week is saved.
- **Acceptance, Kenta:** a vending machine placed by the way home from school: he notices it passing, goes to it the same evening, and his diary says "tried the new vending machine" (tested headless; 3 of 4 seeds by early evening, the fourth before midnight, all with the diary line).
- Fixes found by the fuzz: a slot under another placed object can't be used; no thinking pause while still on a seat; noticing in passing records novelty and a diary note.

Still to do: weather behaviour (umbrellas animating, sheltering, drifting to lit places, snow footprints), the observe camera and the routine-change highlight, reaction animations, the next journal rules and the neighborhood clipboard, more needs (hunger, curiosity, comfort), and the 20-minute fast-forward check for repeated diary lines.

## Tasks
1. **Utility with response curves.**
   - Scores are `weight × Π curve_i(input_i)` (logistic, quadratic, step), with Dave Mark's compensation factor for many considerations.
   - Commitment bonus of +0.1–0.2, a minimum dwell, a hysteresis margin of about 15%, and staggered re-evaluation (0.5–2 s, jittered).
   - Write tests showing no oscillation between two near-equal options.
2. **Needs:** hunger, fatigue, thirst, social, curiosity, comfort (the weather affects this one), plus routine adherence.
3. **Social layer:**
   - Friendship weights between residents.
   - Bows when acquaintances pass, chats in pairs on benches (alternating speech bubbles), waves across the lane.
   - Kids play together.
   - Mitsuko greets every cat by name: she has a cat roster.
4. **Memory:**
   - `favouriteCafe`, `favouriteBench`, `knownPlaces` with novelty decay (half-life about 2 in-game days), `lastSeen[obj]`.
   - Favourites shift when better options appear. This is the visible-adaptation engine.
5. **Weather behaviour:**
   - Umbrellas open and close with an animation.
   - People shelter under awnings and the arch during sudden rain, and drift toward lit lanterns and the kissaten after dark.
   - Kids stay in when it rains hard.
   - Snow: slower walks and footprints that fade (decals).
6. **Story generator.** Template grammar for the story log and per-resident **diaries** in the inspector card ("Tuesday — tried the new bench by the pond. Sunny. Three pigeons."). No LLM: use templates, variables and seeded choice.
7. **Readability:**
   - An "observe" camera button that follows someone.
   - A soft highlight on residents who changed routine because of the player's edit in the last in-game day, with an optional thought-bubble glyph.
   - Exaggerated reaction animations: a little hop, head tilt, pointing.
8. Persist relationships, favourites and diaries in the save.

9. **Start with the thin slice (pulled ahead of prompts 05–07).** Before the full system, ship the cheapest version that makes three people recognisable: Mitsuko, Haru and Rin.
   - `favourite` place memory with decay, and a utility bonus for favourites.
   - Two or three story templates per stage. Mitsuko and a new bench: day 1 she notices it; day 2 she *chooses* it at her afternoon slot over the pond bench ("Mitsuko tries the new bench again"); day 3+ it's her favourite.
   - A "Favourite spot: the bench under the sakura" line on the card.
   - A cat prefers sitting within 2 m of Mitsuko while she's there.
   - Unit tests for each stage. Accessories such as a bag of cat treats are animation work (prompt 09), not this slice.

10. **Follow a resident** (the "observe" button in task 7). Explicitly chosen from the card, cancelled by any pan, zoom or Esc. The camera reframes gently, only when the resident nears the edge of the view. In the palette looks the camera snaps to whole render pixels, so constant following judders by 3–4 screen pixels: offer Follow only in Smooth until the post pass can shift the final image by the leftover sub-pixel fraction.

11. **Journal memories, next steps** (the first version shipped with prompt 04: `src/sim/memories.ts`, three rules, snapshot-rendered FRAME/12 panels in the Journal).
    - More rules, each measurable: "a rainy-day regular" (used in rain on 3 days), "a new routine" (a resident's schedule changed because of an edit, from task 7's highlight data), "the cat's spot" (a cat chose a placed object 3 times).
    - Poses in snapshots: store the pose (sitting, crouching) and re-render it, not just the position.
    - **The neighborhood clipboard.** Real Japanese neighborhoods pass a clipboard notice from house to house. Here a resident physically carries it door to door once an in-game week; when it passes through the player's view, it holds that week's panels (at most four). No badges or notifications: you find it by watching the town. Accessory and walk work belongs to prompt 09.

## Acceptance criteria
- Place a vending machine near the school route. Within one in-game afternoon, Kenta changes his route to pass it, and his diary mentions it.
- Remove Mitsuko's favourite bench. She looks for it the next day (`?` bubble), then adopts another one, and her diary says so.
- 20 minutes of fast-forward shows no oscillation, no idle-forever residents and no repeated identical diary lines.

# 09b — The Noticing Engine

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:

## Goal
Make the hook literally true and checkable: *build a little, watch the neighborhood notice.* Today residents notice an object, use it, grow fond of it and miss it when it's gone. What's missing is **consequence that compounds over days**, and **knowing which edit mattered**. Friendships are static persona data, adjacency between placed things means nothing, and 19 of the 25 placeables are only "admired".

This prompt builds one system with four parts, all deterministic and cheap, with no rollouts and no LLM:
1. a **place field** (William H. Whyte's plaza findings, made computable),
2. **triangulators**: placed things and moments that give strangers something to look at together,
3. a **bond ladder** that grows from repeated co-presence (Milgram's "familiar strangers"), replacing the static friendships,
4. **but-for attribution at the decision point**: a bond is credited to an edit only if masking that edit would have changed the decision that brought the two together.

Run it after 09 (it needs the nod, wave and standing-chat poses) and before 10 (whose weather, wind and festivals become triangulators).

Background and sources are in `RESEARCH-NOTES.md` under "The Noticing Engine". Read that section first.

## Tasks
1. **One scoring function, explained.** Split `Residents.freeTime` into a pure `scoreOptions(agent, view)` that returns every option with its consideration breakdown: base and need curve, distance, crowd, satiation, place memory, social, light and the new field term. Each option also lists the placed object ids that touched its score. The live picker and the attribution check (task 5) both call it, so they can never disagree.
   - Record the mood multipliers drawn per kind at each decision. The re-evaluation reuses them (common random numbers), so masking an edit never changes the dice.
   - A "Why?" view in `?debug` on the resident card: the top three options and their breakdowns.

2. **The place field.** One small grid (the nav grid's 0.5 m cells), recomputed when an edit lands and once an in-game hour (never per frame), with a few channels:
   - **Sun and shade by hour.** Sample the komorebi canopy mask and the buildings' shadow coarsely. Dappled shade is best at noon in summer; sun is best on a winter morning.
   - **Nearness** to trees, water and food (the vending machines, the café).
   - **Shelter:** awnings, the arch, the café terrace.
   - **Light after dark:** lanterns. This replaces today's `lightFactor` radius check.
   - **Prospect-refuge, lite:** a seat with something at its back (a wall, a hedge) and a long view in front scores higher. Use 8–16 rays per seat cell, computed on edit only.
   - **People attract people:** a saturating term with a penalty above the spot's comfortable capacity. Fold today's `crowdFactor` into it.

   The field is one more consideration in `scoreOptions`, not a new AI. The combinations below must emerge, each with a unit test:
   - a bench under a shade tree is preferred at summer noon;
   - a bench by a lantern is preferred at dusk;
   - a bench under an awning is preferred in rain;
   - a bench near the cat bowl draws the cat people.

3. **Triangulators.** Whyte called them the external stimulus that gets strangers talking. Give catalogue entries an `attention` profile: what makes the item emit a **shared-attention window**, for how long, and over what radius. Every admire-only item gets at least one; adjacency starts to matter because emissions combine. Examples:
   - a wind chime rings on a gust (prompt 10's wind);
   - a cat settles on or beside the item;
   - two people are under the same shelter while it rains;
   - a festival lantern is lit;
   - a koi surfaces;
   - the evening bell rings.

   Residents within the radius who are free to attend (a cheap consideration: curiosity, not busy, not working) glance or turn toward it. There is no long detour. Each pair attending the same window registers a **shared moment**.

4. **The bond ladder.** `src/sim/bonds.ts` is pure and has its own tests. Each pair (91 pairs for 14 residents) keeps:
   - the distinct days they were co-present (both visible, within about 4 m, for at least a game minute);
   - their shared moments;
   - the last day they saw each other;
   - their rung, and the edits credited for it (task 5).
   - **Rungs:** unseen → familiar face (co-present on 3 or more distinct days) → nod → greeting → standing chat → friends.
   - **Promotion** past a familiar face needs **both** co-presence and shared moments. Repeated passing alone stalls at a nod.
   - **Fading** uses the place memory's style: a half-life of days, with hysteresis. Bonds fade gently and drop at most one rung a week without contact. Friends never fade below greeting. Nothing ever punishes the player.
   - **Persona `friends` become priors:** affinity speeds progress. A few authored pairs start part-way up, so day one isn't cold: Rin and Sora as friends, Mitsuko and Genzo at greeting.
   - **Behavior per rung**, with the poses from prompt 09:
     - strangers pass by;
     - familiar faces glance;
     - nods at nod and above;
     - bows and waves at greeting and above;
     - standing chats from that rung up;
     - friends seek each other out (`socialFactor` reads the rung).
   - Use a named stream per pair, `bond/<a>|<b>`. Save the ladder with a version and sanitize it field by field, like `places`.

5. **But-for attribution at the decision point.** When a shared moment or a co-presence day is recorded, look at the decision that put each participant there: the stored option scores from task 1. For each placed object that touched that decision, re-run `scoreOptions` with the object masked, reusing the same moods.
   - If the choice flips so they wouldn't have met (another target, or one outside the meeting radius), that edit is a **but-for cause**.
   - If several edits would each have been enough alone, label them **helped** (overdetermination).
   - If masking an edit changes nothing, it gets no credit.
   - Accumulate a per-pair, per-edit share over the moments that built each rung. A rung promotion credits the edits whose share is at least 0.5.
   - This is a pure function of current state: no rollout, no snapshot, microseconds per check. Budget: under 0.2 ms per tick for the whole engine at 14 residents. Measure it.
   - **Undo and removal:** the ledger is sim history, not edit state. Undo still restores a byte-identical world hash. Credits that point to a removed edit are marked "since removed", and the bond stays: "They still nod, though the bench is gone."

6. **Story surfacing.** Small incremental patterns over the ledger feed the Journal, the story feed and the diaries:
   - first nod;
   - rainy-day regulars who become friends;
   - "they've started meeting here after dusk" (a pair keeps meeting at the same spot);
   - a bench outlived.

   A new memory kind, `bond`, gets a three-panel sheet: the edit, a shared moment, the promotion. Captions are short American English templates in `en.ts`, for example: "Because of the wind chime by the bathhouse, Mitsuko and Genzo stopped to listen three evenings running. Today they talked."
   - The resident card gains a short "Neighbors" list (who they nod to, who they're friends with). "Knows" counts people as well as places.
   - On return, a one-line "while you were away" answers who nodded to whom today. It only reports things that really happened.

7. **Optional: a verified badge (QA only).** Once prompt 12 can snapshot the sim, run a short paired rollout (one game hour, the same streams, with and without the edit) in a test or a Worker for the day's top bond story. Log how often the two branches diverge as a QA metric. Gameplay never depends on it. Long rollouts diverge chaotically (pathfinding ties, reservations), so they aren't the attribution method.

## Not doing (and why)
- **A director that steers residents toward stories.** It risks feeling authored. Triangulators already create the moments gently.
- **Paired full-future rollouts as the attribution method.** They diverge, and they need sim snapshots first. See task 7.
- **An LLM for dialogue.** It costs determinism, privacy and tone, and the residents need consequences more than words.
- **A rewrite of the utility AI.** The field and triangulators are considerations in the existing curves.

## Acceptance criteria
- **The hook, as a test.** Seeded run: place a bench and a wind chime by the bathhouse, then fast-forward three days. At least one pair climbs two rungs, and the promotion credits that edit. The same seed with the edit masked from the start: that pair doesn't climb.
- **Metamorphic tests:**
  - moving an unrelated object far away changes no credit;
  - renaming ids changes nothing;
  - a no-op edit consumes no random numbers;
  - with attribution off, the simulation is byte-identical.
- **Determinism:** the same seed and edit log give an identical bond-ledger hash.
- **Diversity:** in a 10-day soak with random edits, no resident appears in more than 25% of bond stories (1 − Σp² is reported).
- **Cost:** the engine adds under 0.2 ms per tick (measured, in the PR).
- **People:** 4 of 5 testers can say *which edit* made a friendship possible, after 15 minutes, without prompting.

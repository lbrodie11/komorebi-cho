# 09c — Birds

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

## Goal
Birds that read as birds at 8–12 render pixels and behave like the town's own: they forage, startle, return, and belong to a season and a habitat. Birds are part of the "notice" loop too: a flock settling on something the player placed is a small story.

**Done already (2026-11-11, `src/sim/birds.ts`):**
- A small rig per bird (body, head, beak, tail, two hinged wings) replaces the single stretching box.
- Wing beats are held frames stepped on twos (12 fps: up, level, down, level). Crows beat, then glide.
- Pigeons peck, turn in small steps and walk-hop; they no longer spin.
- A flock of five tree sparrows hops (never walks) and pecks along the lane and the park edge. They go up together when someone comes within 1.2 m and land a few meters on. They shelter at night and in heavy rain.
- Tests: `tests/birds.test.ts`.

## Tasks
1. **Species with a place and a season.** English names everywhere a player looks.
   - **Tree sparrows:** all year. They fluff up in winter (a slightly rounder body) and dust-bathe in dry summer earth.
   - **Japanese white-eyes:** late winter into spring, in the plum and sakura blossom. Green back, pale belly, a one-pixel white eye-ring. They cling and probe, and rarely touch the ground.
   - **White wagtails:** by the stream and on paving. Black, white and grey; they walk briskly on alternating legs, bob the tail, and fly in dips.
   - **Crows:** they land, not only circle. Roof ridges, the power lines, the bathhouse chimney at dusk. Two calls answer each other.
   - **Barn swallows:** late spring to summer, a nest under the shopping-street arch (a prompt 10 microseason line). Fast low passes over the lane.
   - **Pigeons:** the park and the shrine steps, as now.
2. **Perches as data.** Power lines, roof ridges, eaves, the torii, branch tips and the bench backs get perch points in `layout.ts`, so birds land on things rather than in mid-air.
   - A placed bench, lantern or planter adds perches.
   - A sparrow flock that settles on something the player placed opens a shared-attention window (prompt 09b) and may be a Journal moment.
3. **Silhouette first.** Each species' side view must be recognizable in a 5-second clip at the default zoom in Classic: sparrow (small, round, short tail), wagtail (long tail), white-eye (green, round), crow (big, heavy beak). Add a distant-flock level of detail for birds far off or high up: a two-or-three-pixel V that flaps in two frames.
4. **Sound from the birds themselves.** Today's ambient sparrow chirps and crow calls come from random trees. Move them to the real birds' positions through the spatial placement and the density budget, so the sound follows what you see. Wing flaps on take-off already exist; give the flock one shared flutter, not five.
5. **Behavior details:**
   - birds flee the cats but come back sooner each day the cat ignores them;
   - crumbs by the bakery at opening time draw sparrows;
   - pigeons gather where someone sits on a bench for a while.
   All deterministic, from named streams (`birds/<species>`).
6. **Performance.** Keep birds under 40 draw calls in all: share materials per species, and use `InstancedMesh` per part when a flock passes about 8.
7. **Reduced motion.** Birds still move (they're the town's life), but flights are shorter and lower, take-offs never sweep across the screen, and the distant flock is still.

## Acceptance criteria
- In a 5-second clip at the default zoom in Classic, a viewer can name sparrow, crow and pigeon; with 3× zoom, the white-eye and the wagtail too.
- Walking through a sparrow flock sends it up together, and it lands within 10 m. No bird ever sits inside geometry or floats in the air without a perch.
- Bird sounds pan with the birds (checked with the camera turned 90° and 180°).

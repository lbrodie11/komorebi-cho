# 10 — Time, weather & seasons v2

## Context (shared by every prompt)
You are working on **Komorebi-chō**, a free, web-first, cozy living-diorama game: a fictional Yanaka-style Tokyo shitamachi block on a walnut plinth. It's rendered in three.js (`WebGLRenderer`, r187+) at low resolution through a palette + Bayer-dither post pass, so it reads as pixel art. Residents follow schedules and a utility AI, notice what the player places, and adapt their routines. **All art and audio are generated by code.** No purchased or commissioned assets, no image or audio files.

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` before touching code. Respect the non-negotiables there:
- code-only art
- the palette is law
- no fail states
- never teleport visible residents
- edits are transactions
- determinism via named RNG streams
- respectful, fictional specificity
- a 60 fps budget

Working method:
- Plan first. List the files you will touch and why.
- Implement in small steps. After each one, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.
- Run the dev server and **look**: capture screenshots (Playwright or the browser) at hours 6, 12, 18 and 22, plus rain. Compare them to the screenshots from before your change.
- New logic in `src/sim`, `src/core` or `src/audio/theory.ts` needs unit tests.
- Don't add a dependency unless it removes more complexity than it adds. Say why.
- Finish with a short report:
  - what changed
  - screenshots
  - known gaps
  - which acceptance criteria you verified, and how

## Goal
Weather and seasons that change **affordances**, not just colours, plus small festivals that make the year feel lived.

## Tasks
1. **Weather states:** clear, cloudy, drizzle, rain, downpour, fog, snow, with Markov transitions weighted by season. The `tsuyu` rainy season sits in early summer (seasonal tuning). Wetness lags, puddles grow and shrink, and snow accumulates and melts.
2. **Surfaces:**
   - Wet roughness and darkening already exist. Add puddle masks in depressions, and reflections of lamps in puddles via the mirrored-frame trick from prompt 03.
   - Snow cover: a top-facing material patch (`normal.y > 0.7`) that grows with accumulation, as white palette pixels with blue shadows.
   - Snow footprints.
3. **Affordances:**
   - Awnings and the shotengai arch become shelter.
   - Benches lose desirability when wet.
   - Lanterns attract people after dark.
   - Cats move under eaves when it rains.
   - Kids build a tiny snowman in winter, and it stays until the thaw.
4. **Seasonal decorations** (code-only, with respectful, generic forms):
   - spring: hanami sheets under the sakura, petal drifts in gutters
   - summer: tanabata bamboo with paper strips, wind chimes, and an evening festival with a lantern string and taiko loop
   - autumn: moon-viewing dango on the kissaten terrace, and leaves piling where the caretaker sweeps
   - winter: the New Year kadomatsu by the temple, and steam thicker from the sento chimney
5. **Calendar.** An optional "follow the real date" mode: the season comes from the device date and the time of day can follow the real clock (desktop-companion use). Show the day number and a tiny almanac line in the HUD ("Shōman · sakura falling").
6. **Moon phases** in the post-pass sky, computed from the date.

7. **Lighting gaps left by prompt 03** (each with screenshots at 19:15 clear and in rain):
   - Light pools show in wet streets: a wet-only pool boost, or the mirrored pass extended to wet roads. The post pass already traces reflections for water and puddles (same material), so extend that mask to wet asphalt near light sources rather than adding a new reflection technique; keep the reflections below the kissaten in the hierarchy check.
   - The kissaten door gets one authored downward light (a single `SpotLight`, or a brighter authored pool), so the focal point reads first.
   - Overlapping fixed pools share brightness (the kissaten's two front spills currently add up).
   - A grayscale and silhouette check: `scripts/diffusion-shots.mjs` plus a desaturate step, so the value structure reads without colour.

8. **Colour-direction audit** (2026-11-11 palette review; the palette stays, its use gets disciplined). Already done: a moonlit rim instead of a dark outline where an edge meets a dark night sky (post.ts), the seasonal accent budget (`ACCENT_BUDGET` in lighting.ts), the colour script as comments on each sky key, the subtitle's ink outline. Still to do, each measurable:
   - **Ten acceptance scenes**, same seed, camera and zoom: spring noon, golden hour, blue hour, rainy evening; summer midday, night; autumn golden hour, rainy evening; winter snowy morning, night. Each in Smooth and Classic, at Film Diffusion 0%, 5% and 20%.
   - **Hierarchy check:** convert each frame to greyscale and rank the brightest regions; Sunday Café must rank first at 19:15 in every look and at every diffusion level (small intense streetlamps bloom wider than a big dim window, so check 10–20% especially). The pond's reflections must not become a second focal point.
   - **Placed lights:** ten lanterns clustered must not outshine the café (the √n crowd sharing caps the point lights; check the glowing meshes too).
   - **Winter:** snow roofs, the pale sky and white sign grounds are all near-white; check silhouettes.
   - **Colour blindness:** the vermilion bridge against green trees; the greyscale check covers it.
   - **OKLab experiment:** rebuild the palette LUT with OKLab distance and compare side by side; adopt it only if it visibly helps (watch warm dusk walls snapping to grey).
   - **The benchmark scene:** Sunday Café at blue hour in the rain. New players start at 16:36, so make sure golden hour into blue hour lands in the first minutes too.

**Seen while working on prompt 11 (check first):** at dusk in the rain, the lit shop windows show as large flat yellow blocks: the glass reads as a flat, saturated rectangle rather than a window, and the effect is strongest on the florist and the ramen shop. Main does the same (compared on the same frame, 16:48 in rain, before and after prompt 08's changes), so it predates this work. Likely suspects: the rain/wetness boost on window emission or the wet-glass reflection, pushing the glass to the top of the palette with no variation; or the wet-street mirror reflecting the windows back over the facades. The fix should keep the windows warm and readable as windows (frames, a little variation behind the glass) and keep the hierarchy check (the kissaten still the brightest thing at 19:15).

## Acceptance criteria
- A 30-minute fast-forward through a year shows all four seasons, at least five weather states and every festival, with no visual glitches.
- Residents measurably change behaviour in rain (path heat-map comparison in the PR).
